# Module Outro
{: .no_toc }

*Video duration: 1:21*

That closes the core curriculum. Across nine modules you've built a mental model — the ATWPC stack — for looking at any Claude-powered system and asking, in order, whether it should be an agent at all, what it's allowed to do, how its steps are organized, how it's instructed, and what it actually sees. This module didn't add a sixth layer; it showed you the seams between the five you already know: patterns like the do-nothing option and structure-over-prose that recur across layers, antipatterns like tool sprawl and assumed human review that recur just as reliably, and a revisit of the ten takeaways now backed by five modules of concrete detail instead of a preview.

One thing left: proving it under exam conditions. The next module is the Practice Exam — a full-length, timed simulation spanning all five domains at roughly the same proportions as the real CCA-F, with scenario questions built the way this course has been training you to read them. Treat it as a diagnostic, not a victory lap: work through every question deliberately, review every wrong answer against the domain it was actually testing, and come back to the relevant module here if a gap opens up. You're ready for it.

Before you move on, it's worth being honest with yourself about where you actually stand, rather than where you'd like to stand. Nine modules of content is a lot to hold at once, and it's completely normal for one or two layers to feel noticeably shakier than the others — maybe Context management's compaction and provenance mechanics didn't fully stick, or maybe the Tool layer's error-response design details are fuzzier than the rest. That's exactly the kind of gap the Practice Exam is built to surface cleanly, since a cluster of missed questions in one domain is a far more reliable signal than your own gut sense of what you know well.

If you do have a strong hunch about a weak spot already, this is a good moment to skim that module's Key Distinctions page and quiz one more time before you sit the full exam — not to re-learn the material from scratch, but to refresh the vocabulary you'll need to move quickly when a scenario question in that domain shows up. Fast, confident elimination of wrong answers matters as much as knowing the right one, and that speed comes from familiarity you build through repetition, not from a single read-through.
